From crowd to community

Attendees: 5,000+

Feedback responses: 700-800 (about 15%)

Community sign-ups: about 150 (roughly 20% of respondents)

Problem

A direct-selling company had a new healthy-snack range and no customer evidence on which products to put forward.

Initiative

Use an existing associate promotion event to run a secret blind tasting, so the range could be tested with thousands of real buyers at once.

What I did

  • Led planning, creatives, marketing and pre-launch secrecy.
  • Coordinated 3 local influencers and managed expenses, finishing within budget.
  • Built and led 40-50 volunteers across tasting, backend, product explanation, crowd handling, feedback collection and marketing.
  • Personally collected video testimonials on the floor.

Result

  • An analyst ranked the range into a top 5 and a bottom 3.
  • After a management discussion, the most-liked products were shortlisted and 1-2 were held back for taste rework. The NPD team heard feedback live.
  • Contact details seeded a pre-launch community of about 150.
  • The launch plan was later paused at company level.

Limitation

Over 90% said they would refer others, but respondents were existing associates, so I treated that as weak evidence. The most-avoided items were maida and oily or fried foods.

What I’d do differently

Add non-associate tasters so the sample reflects ordinary shoppers, not only fans of the company.

Key learning

A good product isn’t enough. You have to start from what customers need and want, and know whose opinion you are measuring.
